## Summary

Two things, in the order they depend on each other.

1. **Hoenn map data and pathfinding.** A generator beside the HGSS one reads a `pret/pokeruby` checkout and writes `Data/RS` in the HGSS container: one kind byte per tile with HGSS's meanings, plus an elevation byte. Script state is in from the first version: NPCs that exist only while a flag is clear, and script trigger tiles with their variables. Trainers' lines of sight are not part of the route (owner, 2026-11-15): a trainer is an NPC like any other, and meeting one is a stop. The bot reads the flags and variables once per navigation. `HasOverworldNavigation` becomes true for Ruby and Sapphire.
2. **Training.** The Wild Encounter action's Train Type Standard and Switch open to Ruby and Sapphire: fight with a move, the level-up box, new moves, evolution, the in-battle switch, and the trip to a Pokémon Center and back when the party is spent. `HasTraining` becomes true.

The spike generated the data for 370 maps (53 KB gzipped), walked routes from it on the emulator at the first try (across a map connection, through a door, over an exit mat, the length of Route 102), healed at two Pokémon Centers, saw a script trigger stay quiet because its variable did not match, and drove every training screen from Lv 7 through an evolution at Lv 16 from memory.

A trainer battle is never fought by the bot: it goes to Waiting, pings Discord, and waits for Resume (owner, 2026-11-15).

## Goals

- G1: Ruby and Sapphire map data in the HGSS byte-per-tile container, generated from the decomp checkout with one command.
- G2: routes that respect the save's story state: flags and variables read from RAM decide which NPCs and script tiles are live (owner, 2026-11-15: done in the first version, not later).
- G3: the walk to a Pokémon Center, the heal, and the walk back, with every press confirmed and every wait bounded.
- G4: Standard and Switch training with the same choices as HGSS.
- G5: reuse. The HGSS search, navigator and level-up decisions where they fit; the differences in the profile, the address table and the macro registry.
- G6: a trainer battle stops the bot for the operator, with a ping.

## Non-goals

- Using an HM in the field, and reading from RAM which ones the party can use (the moves and the badges). The data and the search model every HM (FR-4a), and the capability is a value passed in; until the reads and the field-move step exist it is empty, so routes that need an HM are not taken (owner, 2026-11-15: all the HMs in the data and the search, the capability addresses later).
- Flash. It lights a cave and changes no route.
- Ice, slides, currents, rails, cracked floors and the Mach Bike slopes as walkable ground. They are one kind and the search treats it as blocked.
- Secret Bases, the Safari Zone, the Battle Tower, the Trick House, contests.
- Fighting trainers, and routing round their lines of sight. Meeting one is a stop (FR-10).
- Pickup and Chained Encounter on Ruby and Sapphire.
- Building the flag gates for HGSS. The design is shared with the [HGSS pathfinder spec](pathfinder-maps-flags-hms.md); this feature builds the pieces flavor-neutral so HGSS can adopt them, and does not regenerate `Data/HGSS`.

## Design

### The tile byte and the second plane

The owner's HGSS data is a byte per tile holding a kind, compressed as a whole. Hoenn keeps that exactly, so the search's tile rules carry over, and adds what Hoenn needs that a kind cannot say: the elevation. Two planes of bytes compress to 53 KB for the region. Packing kind and elevation into one byte was considered: the kinds already use the top bit for blocked and need five bits, elevation needs four.

### Script state

Two kinds of thing on the map depend on the story, and the decomp names the flag or variable for each:

| On the map | Lives while | Data | Effect on a route |
|---|---|---|---|
| An NPC with an event flag (0x12) | the flag is clear | 545 objects, all resolved | blocked while live, free after |
| A script trigger tile (0x14) | its variable holds its value | 194 tiles, 162 resolved | cost 200 while live |

The spike saw both rules hold on the emulator, and a trigger whose variable did not match did nothing.

Trainers are left out of the route on purpose (owner, 2026-11-15). The spike's prototype marked each trainer's line of sight as costly ground and the first draft kept that; the owner's call is that it is not the pathfinder's business. A trainer who sees the player starts a battle, the bot stops, pings and waits (FR-10), the operator wins, and after that the trainer never challenges again. What does matter for a route is the trainer as an object: one that walks a path can be in the way at any moment, and a beaten trainer still stands on its tile and blocks it. Both are covered by treating a trainer as any other NPC: its tile blocked, its walking range marked.

This is the design of the [HGSS pathfinder spec](pathfinder-maps-flags-hms.md)'s second part, which is specified and not built. Building the flag set, the gated kinds and the side file flavor-neutral here means HGSS gets them by regenerating its data later.

### Maps joined by connections

HGSS's outdoor world is a few stitched maps with one coordinate space. Hoenn's towns and routes each have their own, and the game swaps the map and the coordinates as the player steps over an edge. So an edge between maps is a connection (walk off the side) or a warp (a door, or a mat with a direction). The `M` action does not mind: it waits for a coordinate, and the coordinate after the edge is the new map's, so one macro can run across it.

### Training by rule

The wild-encounter battle event already knows the action menu, the text wait and the free field. Training adds six waits, each a byte or a task state found in the spike: the move list, the level-up box (a counter that reads 1 then 2), the move prompt (a tracker and the battle script pointer), the forget-a-move screen (a cursor, the summary's input task and the palette fade), the evolution task (its state and sub-state, with the battle window for its text waits), and the party screen for the switch. HGSS's `LevelUpFlow` is not called on RS: the game's own state decides what comes next (one box per level, the move offered in `gMoveToLearn`, the evolution scene starting by itself), and the one decision reused is the Discord question `LevelUpFlow` asks, `SendNewMoveDecisionAndWaitAsync` (a slot, or decline). The move is chosen by HGSS's damage picker from the Gen 3 party records (T1 plan, #430).

As built, the battle event waits on `GameSnapshot`s from `RsStateReader` (seven direct reads in one request on one frame) and reads the rest itself in one-frame blocks. The new waits follow that: each becomes a screen or a value on the snapshot, or a block read beside it, and none is a loop of single reads. `BattleMoveList` and `BattleTurn` are already classified.

### Addresses per game

Until now one table has served Ruby and Sapphire, because the bot read RAM and battle code, which are identical. The nurse, the Yes/No menu, the party menu and the evolution scene are field-side code, four bytes apart between the two games at 1.2. The table gains a small per-game part, keyed by the game code in the ROM header that `RomInfo` already carries.

## What diverged

1. **FR-10, the trainer stop** (owner, 2026-11-17: "Detect the speech"). The game sets no battle type until A is pressed at the trainer's intro speech, so a bot that presses nothing never sees 0x0008. The stop is read while the speech is up, in one read: the field locked, the battle bit clear, the script status not 2, `gTrainerBattleOpponent` (0x0202FF5E) not 0, and that trainer's flag (0x500 + id) clear. The navigator hands a lock over once the field has stayed locked on the same map and tile for `GameProfile.SeekLockHandOff` (4 s); Spin and Run hand it over once the field has been away from free for 4 s and is locked at that moment. The sentence names the map ("A trainer battle started on Route102."). Resume after a trainer stop waits for the free field and parks again if it does not come (10 s), with no second message. The 0x0008 check stays as a backstop. Known limit, not read on the emulator: after a lost trainer battle the id stays and the flag stays clear, so the next script met while seeking or walking reads as a challenge.
2. **FR-12, addresses per game.** "Ruby four bytes lower" and "they also move between revisions" were wrong as rules. From pret's symbol tables (symbols branch, 78b1990), five members differ by game: `WaitForAorBPress` and `PartyMenuMainCallback2` (Ruby four bytes lower) and the three yes/no script addresses `DeleteMoveYesNo`, `StopLearningYesNo` and `UseNextPokemonYesNo` (Ruby 0x70 higher; the third added by #475), which are battle-side. Everything else used is the same on Ruby and Sapphire, and revision 1 (1.1) equals revision 2 (1.2) for every address used. So revision 1 is not refused; a train mode is refused only on an unknown game code, where the per-game part is null. The 1.1 values are decomp only.
3. **FR-1 to FR-4, the data.** 394 maps, not 370: the Secret Base interiors are built, though nothing routes into them. Puddles and shallow water are free ground, not Surf. The side file is `events.json.gz` (NFR-1). A warp is written only where the tile's behaviour in the layout fires one (118 dropped as non-warp tiles, plus 41 to unknown maps, 7 off their own map, 2 landing off their destination), and it carries the tile the player ends on after the game's arrival step, not the warp index. Recorded gaps: the 42 warps a script opens with `setmetatile` (Petalburg Gym's rooms, the Trick House exits, the Elite Four doors, the Abandoned Ship, one Lilycove door) are plain ground in the data; Rusturf Tunnel's two rocks stay 0x10 after the story removes them; a warp's or trigger's elevation is not carried (they fire at any elevation). The nurse is not in the side file; the counter tile (7, 4) facing her at (7, 2) is the same in all 15 Pokémon Centers.
4. **FR-7.** Tile-level Dijkstra across maps in `RouteSearch`; no graph and no `PathfindingService` for RS. Plan times on the committed data: 1 to 5 ms to a Pokémon Center, 9 ms for every reachable tile with an empty mask. With Strength the full-region search takes about 24 s, since every boulder arrangement is a state: a recorded limit for the field-move feature, not reached now because the navigator passes an empty mask. The search uses HGSS's waterfall rule, but on RS a waterfall tile is a forced current south: also for that feature.
5. **FR-8.** `RunHold` is joined to every segment's own first step, not the first segment's only: B does not stay held across macros (observed 2026-11-17). New rule R3: a run that crosses a warp or connection onto a coordinate the player already shows is cut at the landing and waits on the other axis, or, when neither axis is new, ends on the warp tile's own coordinate, with the next run starting from the landing.
6. **FR-9.** A segment ends when its macro has ended and the player is free on its tile (callback 1 reads 0 with the lock 0 for a few frames after a warp, which is not free). The last segment also needs the running state and the tile transition both 0. The first QA run of `rs-train-heal` found this: the hunt's first turn was sent as the walk's last step was finishing and became a step ("The player moved while turning."). Fixed in N4c (#469) and run five times since. The hand-off clock counts only a lock with the map and tile unchanged: a door locks for at most 70 frames, a wild battle's start 93, a trainer indefinitely.
7. **FR-11, the nurse.** The Yes is pressed only with `Task_HandleYesNoInput` active and its counter (task `data[2]`) at 5: an A sent earlier is dropped (seen on both games; the spike's dropped press). The talk needs the tile transition 0 and the facing up. The last wait is the script's own `WaitForAorBPress` with status 0 and mode 2, after the Yes; the native alone is stale. Six presses, each sent once, by a rule loop, so a Pokérus page would be pressed as a page break.
8. **FR-13 to FR-15.** HGSS's `LevelUpFlow` is not called on RS (it reads HGSS's battle parser and presses touch macros); the game's own state decides (`expGetterMonId`, `gMoveToLearn`), and the one decision reused is the Discord new-move question. A move prompt is recognised only when the tracker and the battle script pointer agree, because the tracker is stale after use. The forget-a-move screen takes a press only with its input task running and the palette fade off; the cursor cannot gate input. Every press with a confirming value goes through `PressConfirmedAsync`: once, and once more only when the snapshot never moved within the repair wait (1 s). `Gen3PokemonParser` now sets PP (it never did); the damage picker takes the generation (3 for RS).
9. **FR-16.** B is pressed in the evolution scene only as the No at its "Delete a move?". The scene's text waits are the battle window at a button wait; its first text does not wait.
10. **FR-17.** The party list takes keys only once callback 2 is `CB2_PartyMenuMain` and the fade is off (the task alone is up four or five frames too early); SHIFT only once the popup task shows. Nothing is pressed and no party record is read from SHIFT to the next action menu (13.3 s; the two party records are swapped in memory for about 22 frames). The switch is read at that menu from `gBattlerPartyIndexes`.
11. **FR-18a.** Five capability members (`HasChainedEncounter`, `HasStaticEncounter`, `HasHeadbuttTrees`, `HasPickup`, `HasWildRunChecks`) carry what the two capabilities used to gate; the "no training" greying and its sentences are deleted. A finished RS hunt walks back to its start tile before Complete, as HGSS does, a flee-only hunt included (Spin: no walk; Run: back along the line).
12. **FR-18b.** No Gen 3 battle parser: the party record carries HP and PP every turn (observed). The constructor's rules are narrowed to match, and a navigator part (`CreateNavigatorEvent`) is on the profile.
13. **QA.** States are reached by RAM writes; the second party member is a copy of the lead built in RAM, not caught. The Discord new-move question is answered by a click in Discord (the operator's, or the orchestrator's in a browser signed in as the owner): the QA backend is logged in, so every prompt is posted, and a bot cannot click its own buttons. `rs-nav-trainer` has no Spin variant (none of the trainer's sight tiles is grass), and its "walk toward Petalburg" is the walk back after the win. `rs-train-switch-battler-spent`'s "fainted in battle" asserted the bot's stale stop at "Use next POKéMON?" while the owner's question was open; since #475 `fainted` asserts the flee and the heal its row describes, and `fainted-run` and `fainted-stop` force the flee to fail by the wild Pokémon's battle Speed, as the readings did. `fainted-stop` needs the Run after the send-out to fail, which about 1 in 8 runs it does not (30 is added to the escape roll per try); such a run is run again.
14. **The task split.** The suggested nine tasks became 24: N1b (warp data), N3a and N3b, N4a to N4c, N5 (the trainer stop, split from N4 so it did not share a file with T1), T1r, T1a and T1b, T3a and T3b, and Q1a to Q1h were split out or found by planners, reviews and the first QA runs. Five emulator research rounds (the trainer speech, training R1 to R8, the navigator W1 to W9 and N1 to N6, the switch S0 to S11) ran before the plans that depended on them were approved; each is a section of the research doc.
15. **Limits recorded, not fixed.** One frame after arriving by stairs reads free with both movement bytes 0 while a scripted step runs (harmless unless a route ends on a stair landing's walk-off tile). In the flee-only hunt (Flee mode) "Use next POKéMON?" is not recognised and ends in the 30 s stale stop; training answers it (#475, FR-18). Log lines count slots and moves from 0 while the dashboard counts from 1. `RsBattleEventTests` takes about 85 s (a fixed poll interval over scripted battles).
